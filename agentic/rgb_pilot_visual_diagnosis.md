Reviewed 24 actual CURRENT RGB images (4 per decision), all six candidate JSONs and execution reports. Verified SHA256 of every image in the six model request manifests, including C/D historical images. Visual inspection here covers the current images; history images were hash checked, not all independently visually inspected. Source links and hashes are in BCD_VISUAL_EVIDENCE.json. ## Findings

- **Not a terminal close-up cropping failure.** B decision01 cuts the upper part of the wrist at the image top but both fingertips and bottle are visible. B02, C02/C03 and D18/D19 show the hand and fingertips inside the close-up; full-arm overview covers the entire arm. High arm links being outside the close-up is intentional, and is supplemented by overview. This does not prove initial cropping had no effect, but the last observations do contain the contact-relevant fingers.
- **Top-down occlusion remains real.** The hand occludes most of the bottle in every reviewed top view, leaving a thin brown edge to the right. Thus this camera is weak evidence for exact centering; in-frame COM coverage is not equivalent to a visible grasp target.
- **Wrist view supplies an offset warning, but not a guaranteed calibrated action direction.** The bottle cap is right/below the visible finger gap and grows as the hand descends. At C03 and D19 it approaches/overlaps the lower visible finger region. The near camera and perspective exaggerate these image relationships. The two visible fingers provide an opening-axis reference; neither that nor image horizontal/vertical alone identifies the sign and metric magnitude of local X/Y without camera extrinsics or an empirical motion-response probe. The policy is deliberately not given these extrinsics. Our private rig knows the mapping; that is not evidence that the model inferred it.
- **The model notices ambiguity but postpones correction.** B02 explicitly mentions a lateral offset, then avoids correction because it may be perspective. C03 notes the cap nearer the lower finger and the overhead brown sliver, but requests another 25 mm +Z descent. D18/D19 likewise notices the right/below cap offset and continues 20 mm +Z, planning to reassess later. This is observable output behavior, not a claim about hidden reasoning.
- **Collision is a finger approach collision, not the old two-bottle palm collision.** Terminal safety records identify fr3_leftfinger versus the brown bottle in B/C/D. Final requested descents are B 108 mm (executed 1.2167/2 s), C 25 mm (0.1167/1.5 s), D 20 mm (0.7583/2 s). Each still requests full opening and no rotation. A smaller step increased observation opportunities but did not resolve the remaining lateral alignment in this pilot.

## Limits and next diagnostic

The pictures alone cannot measure exact 3D fingertip clearance or identify all collision causes. The apparent gap in an oblique projection is not a collision-free 3D corridor. Do not equate no visible contact with actual contact absence. Combine this report with the independent numerical fingertip/bottle/contact trajectory audit.

The evidence supports a specific hypothesis: lateral alignment uncertainty was detected but repeatedly deferred while descending. It does not establish that RGB-only control is impossible, that axes were numerically inverted, or that a new camera alone would solve the task. A separately declared, safe early motion-response probe or a more informative fixed lateral view could test this hypothesis; neither was run in this audit. Do not introduce simulator-derived signed corrections while retaining the strict-RGB label.
