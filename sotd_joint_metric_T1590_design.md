# Scope and references
This is a neural local nonconvex constrained optimization in a finite KL-Fisher gradient subspace. It does not inherit the finite convex distribution-level global optimality theorem in DERIVATION_PACKAGE.md. Rewards are finite-horizon shaped returns, costs are original undiscounted episode totals. No cross-state transport: W2 is conditional on the same old states and acts on diagonal auxiliary Gaussians. Inner exploration-Gaussian cost surrogates and outer deterministic sampled episode-excess checks concern different kernels and risk functionals; the outer screen is engineering validation, not a safety certificate.
Gaussian W2 and Wasserstein trust regions are established, not novelty here. The diagonal formula uses differences of standard deviations, not variances. See Peyré/Cuturi, [Computational Optimal Transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00567), Gaussian treatment (user references2.41/2.42); [OT-TRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11137) develops trust regions based on optimal transport discrepancies; [Differentiable Trust Region Layers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09207) and [Metric-aware Trust Region Methods / WPO-SPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14133) are relevant prior approaches. Primary abstract pages checked for this design. We do not claim to implement these full algorithms or their guarantees.
The experiment tests whether changing the distance inside an otherwise shared reward/cost subproblem improves the measured safety-reward frontier. Shared D isolates the trust-region constraint rather than simultaneously changing preconditioning. Anchor calibration matches units at one fixed training direction only; three shared scales expose sensitivity, not uniform equivalence. Best-safe-KL comparison is a prespecified exploratory developmental comparison, not a powered superiority test or evaluation-free model selection guarantee.
